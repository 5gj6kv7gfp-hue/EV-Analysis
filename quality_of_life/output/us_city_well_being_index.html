What drives well-being

The resident measures and their weights come from a survey-weighted regression of life satisfaction in the CDC's Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), 2023–2024, with age, sex, race, education, and state-by-year controls. Place factors are not in that survey, so their weights rest on published studies.

Social connection carries the most weight
Lacking emotional support and loneliness are the two largest effects in the model, and they also vary a lot between cities. This matches a long research record: social relationships are among the strongest correlates of happiness (Helliwell & Putnam, 2004) and of survival (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010).
Mental and physical health come next
Frequent mental distress is the single biggest predictor of being dissatisfied with life (+11 percentage points). Clark et al. (The Origins of Happiness, 2018) find mental illness explains more of the variation in life satisfaction than income does.
Work and money matter, with diminishing returns
Unemployment hurts beyond the lost pay (Clark & Oswald, 1994), and so does material hardship: not covering rent or utilities, or lacking transportation. Income itself has a small, log-shaped effect (Kahneman & Deaton, 2010). People also compare themselves with neighbors (Luttmer, 2005), so a city's average income adds little to average well-being. That is why purchasing power is off by default.
Place conditions: safety, commute, air, climate
Commuting is the least enjoyed part of most people's day (Kahneman et al., 2004), and longer commutes lower life satisfaction (Stutzer & Frey, 2008). Crime lowers well-being through victimization and fear (Cohen, 2008). Fine-particle pollution lowers reported happiness (Levinson, 2012). Americans prefer mild climates, around 65 °F (Albouy et al., 2016), but climate effects on well-being are modest, so it gets a small weight.

How the index is built

  1. Cities. Every Census place with 200,000+ residents in the 2024 American Community Survey: 130 cities, measured within city limits.
  2. Resident measures. CDC PLACES age-adjusted estimates for each city. Each domain's score is the life satisfaction the BRFSS model predicts from the city's prevalence of those conditions.
  3. Resident weights. A domain's weight is proportional to how much its predicted life satisfaction varies across the 130 cities. Social connection varies most (28%), then physical health (18%), economic security (16%), and mental health (8%). Together they make up 70%.
  4. Place weights. Safety 15%, commute 7%, air quality 4%, climate 4%, set from the studies above. Safety averages log homicide and log violent-crime rates.
  5. Scaling. Each domain is standardized across the cities, combined with the weights, and rescaled so the 130-city average is 100 and one standard deviation is 10.
  6. Cost of living. BEA 2024 Regional Price Parities for the city's metro area (US = 100). The housing component (17% of spending) is rescaled by the city's median rent relative to its metro, so Irvine and Santa Ana, which share a metro, get different costs.
  7. Robustness. Rankings barely move under other weightings. Spearman correlation with the default ranking: equal weights 0.97, resident measures only 0.98, a 50/50 resident/place split 0.98, adding purchasing power at 10% 0.99.

Limits

  • PLACES numbers are model-based estimates driven partly by each city's age, race, and education mix. The index describes how a city's residents are doing, which mixes what the place does for people with who chooses to live there.
  • No public source measures life satisfaction city by city, so the index predicts well-being rather than measuring it directly.
  • For TX, FL, TN, WA, and KY, social-needs measures come from the 2022 survey, calibrated to 2023. For CO, OR, PA, and SD (7 cities), they are imputed from other PLACES measures; these cities are flagged.
  • Crime rates use the reporting agency's own population. Las Vegas, Enterprise, and Spring Valley share the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department; Honolulu's figure covers the whole island.
  • The weights for place conditions are judgment informed by the literature, not estimates. Use the sliders to test your own.
